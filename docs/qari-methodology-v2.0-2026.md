# Queer Atlas Risk Index (QARI) · Methodology 2.0 · 2026

Status: methodology and data-model release, with an initial public profile cohort. QARI 1.x remains visibly labelled while each remaining country completes the QARI 2.0 review workflow.

## Purpose

QARI 2.0 is a country-level LGBTQ+ travel-planning signal. It identifies the type and current evidence of exposure; it does not predict personal safety, label a population, or make a city safe or unsafe.

## Four dimensions

1. **Legal conditions**: laws, formal restrictions and statutory penalties.
2. **State repression**: documented actions by police, prosecutors, courts or other authorities.
3. **Digital exposure**: documented LGBTIQ+-specific digital methods, capacity and restrictions.
4. **Social and civil reality**: violence, harassment, practical protection, reporting and civil-society conditions.

Digital methods and state actions are recorded separately. A single incident has one primary scoring owner so it cannot inflate an aggregate through double counting.

## Non-compensatory result

QARI 2.0 has no weighted country average. The public headline is the highest level triggered by an eligible rule: Lower, Moderate, High or Extreme. Countries at the same headline level are compared only through an explanatory profile: number of independently elevated dimensions, rule trail, evidence availability, recency and trend. The interface must not present that ordering as a precise risk measurement.

## Evidence grades

- **A**: legislation, court decision, UN body or ILGA World.
- **B**: established human-rights organisation, or corroborated reporting from independent credible outlets.
- **C**: a single credible but uncorroborated source. This creates a monitored finding only and cannot trigger a public rule.

Every triggered rule retains its rule code, source, evidence grade, event date, review date and expiry review date.

## Evidence availability

Each dimension is marked Good, Limited, Opaque or Unknown. No direct evidence is never translated to lower risk. In opaque environments, restrictive laws can independently determine the public level while missing practice evidence remains visibly unknown.

## Freshness and six-month review

Every profile has a mandatory review date six calendar months after its review date. Material legal changes or credible A/B evidence of a worsening condition trigger an immediate review. Legal rules remain active until confirmed changed, repealed or invalidated. Event-based rules become due for re-evaluation after 24 months; they do not automatically disappear or lower a profile.

## Release gate

A QARI 2.0 increase to High or Extreme, or a High/Extreme state-repression finding, requires a linked editorial safety review for every published city in that country. The review covers city guide copy, venue and event visibility, search/SEO copy and translations before a public risk update is complete.

## Initial public cohort

The public QARI 2.0 cohort is defined in the reviewed country-profile data and replaces a 1.x profile only after eligible rules and a source trail have been recorded. High- or Extreme-repression findings require guide, translation, venue-access and search/SEO checks before publication. Every subsequent country must pass the same release gate; publication is never inferred from a numerical result alone.

## Public presentation

The product must show the headline level, four dimension levels, evidence availability, trend, review date, next review date, sources and triggered rule codes. It must not describe a country as safe or turn lack of evidence into reassurance.
