# Queer Atlas Risk Index (QARI) · Methodology v1.0

Status: Phase 0 editorial pilot  
Review date: 2026-08-18  
Scope: Spain, Canada, Germany, Brazil, Namibia, Turkey, Egypt and Russia

## Purpose

QARI is a travel-planning signal for LGBTQ+ people. It is not a declaration that a destination is safe or unsafe for every person. Identity, neighbourhood, time, current events and individual circumstances can change exposure substantially.

The scale runs from 0 (lower documented risk) to 100 (extreme documented risk). Queer Atlas uses **Lower risk**, never **Safe**, for the lowest band.

## Formula

`QARI = (Legal risk × 0.35) + (Social reality × 0.40) + (Digital & enforcement risk × 0.25)`

All three inputs are risk scores from 0–100:

- **Legal risk (35%)**: criminalisation, expression and association restrictions, discrimination protection, hate-crime law, family recognition, legal gender recognition and healthcare restrictions.
- **Social reality (40%)**: documented violence or harassment, public climate, practical visibility, reporting conditions and recent credible local evidence.
- **Digital & enforcement risk (25%)**: dating-app entrapment or targeting, device checks, censorship, morality or cybercrime enforcement, police conduct and the risk attached to reporting an incident.

The weighted result is rounded to the nearest whole number. An evidence-based risk floor may raise the result; it may never lower it.

## Risk floors

- Death penalty prescribed or legally possible: minimum 90.
- Active imprisonment-based criminalisation: minimum 80.
- Criminalisation targeting gender identity or expression: minimum 80.
- Documented de facto criminalisation through other laws: minimum 72.
- Systematic dating-app entrapment by authorities: minimum 68.

Risk floors require a source-level editorial finding. Missing information never defaults to zero.

## Bands

- **0–20 · Lower risk**: strong baseline, while normal situational awareness remains relevant.
- **21–45 · Moderate risk**: meaningful variation in social reality, protection or practical exposure.
- **46–70 · High risk**: legal, social or enforcement conditions require deliberate planning.
- **71–100 · Extreme risk**: severe legal or enforcement exposure; identity and digital traces may carry material risk.
- **Not yet verified**: no numerical score is shown.

## Confidence

Confidence is independent of the score:

- **High**: recent primary or authoritative sources cover all three axes and material claims agree.
- **Medium**: all axes have credible support, but recent local evidence is limited or conditions vary.
- **Low**: important evidence is old, incomplete or conflicting. Low-confidence rows should normally remain unpublished.

## Source and editorial rules

1. Every published profile needs at least one source for each axis.
2. Legal claims should prioritise ILGA World, ILGA-Europe, legislation, court decisions and government material.
3. Social claims should distinguish reported incidents from prevalence; raw hate-crime counts are not compared across countries without accounting for reporting differences.
4. Travel advisories may support practical risk findings but do not determine the whole score.
5. Community reports are not part of v1.0. When introduced, they must be aggregated, moderated and kept separate from verified editorial evidence.
6. A score change requires a dated review and retained source trail.
7. QARI does not infer personal identity, store a visitor's selected context or promise safety.

## Phase 0 pilot results

| Country | Legal | Social | Digital | QARI | Band | Confidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Spain | 12 | 18 | 8 | 13 | Lower risk | High |
| Canada | 9 | 23 | 8 | 14 | Lower risk | High |
| Germany | 12 | 24 | 8 | 16 | Lower risk | High |
| Brazil | 16 | 48 | 34 | 33 | Moderate risk | Medium |
| Namibia | 52 | 55 | 30 | 48 | High risk | Medium |
| Turkey | 68 | 64 | 70 | 67 | High risk | Medium |
| Egypt | 86 | 85 | 92 | 87 | Extreme risk | High |
| Russia | 92 | 90 | 96 | 92 | Extreme risk | High |

These are country baselines. A later city layer may adjust social and digital context, but it must not override a national legal or enforcement risk floor.
